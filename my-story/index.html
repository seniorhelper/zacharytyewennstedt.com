The long version

Every scar is a teacher.
Every failure is the price of wisdom.

I have paid that price more times than I would like to itemize. Here is the whole road, including the parts most consultant bios quietly leave out.

WHERE IT STARTS

Born in Fremont, Nebraska

Family scattered across Omaha, Lincoln, Fremont and Schuyler. Nebraska is not a market I researched; it is people I am related to.

That matters more than it sounds. When I work with a business in Omaha I am not reading a demographic report — I know how people there talk about money, how long they take to decide, and how badly an out-of-state agency reads to them. Half of marketing is knowing what will sound fake to the person receiving it.

GROWING UP

Raised in Boulder. Fairview High.

Colorado has been home ever since. Kindergarten in Colorado Springs, the rest of it along the Front Range.

Boulder in those years was a strange place to be a kid who liked selling things. It is a town full of very smart people who are slightly embarrassed by commerce, and I was the opposite — genuinely delighted by the idea that you could persuade someone to want something. I never grew out of it. I just got better at doing it honestly.

FIVE YEARS

Fort Collins and Colorado State

I left before the degree to build a business. I have never once claimed otherwise, and I am not going to start on my own website.

People expect that to be a confession. It is not. I would rather you know exactly what you are hiring: someone who learned this by doing it with his own money on the line, not someone with a credential who has never had to make payroll. If a degree is what you need, there are excellent people who have one.

What those five years did give me was Fort Collins — a market I understand from the inside, including how completely a university town's economy breathes with the academic calendar.

BEFORE ANY OF THIS

Selling, then running sales teams

Sales manager for multi-million dollar plumbing companies. Then managing national remodeling companies and the teams inside them.

This is the part of my background that actually makes me useful, and it is the part most marketing people do not have. I have sat at kitchen tables. I have watched a good product lose to a worse one because the other company called back first. I have trained reps, built presentations, written objection answers and then listened to them fail in the field and rewritten them.

Everything I do now — websites, search, AI, automation — is downstream of that. They are all just distribution for a conversation I already knew how to have.

2012

Eye To Ad Media

Started the agency and never stopped. 60 Google reviews at 5.0, A+ BBB accredited, still answering my own phone.

Fourteen years in this industry is long enough to have watched three or four complete reinventions of how people find things. Directories to search. Search to local. Local to mobile. And now search to whatever this is — AI answering before the links load.

The companies that survive each shift are the ones that were not gaming the previous one. That is a large part of why I build the way I do.

AND ALSO

I run companies that are not marketing companies

A bath remodeling business. Hundreds of keyword domains. A virtual reality shopping mall. Local buy-sell-trade groups with over 200,000 members.

This is the unfair advantage, and I will say plainly that it is unfair. Every tactic I sell you has already been run on a company where the money was mine. When a channel stops working I find out because my own phone goes quiet, not because a client complains six months later.

It also means I know what it feels like on your side of the table — the specific stomach-drop of paying for marketing and watching nothing happen. I have been there with my own money, and I have been there as the customer of agencies who took it.

“I have been burned by agencies. I have made my own expensive mistakes. Both of those are the reason I am useful to you now.”

The one that cost most

Paying for a channel for nearly a year because the reporting looked healthy and I never checked whether any of it turned into revenue. Traffic was up. Everything was up. Nothing was ringing.

The one I see everywhere

Building something beautiful before proving anyone wanted it. It is the most enjoyable way to waste money that exists, and I will talk you out of it if you let me.

The one that still stings

Trusting a vendor because they were confident rather than because they were right. Confidence is free. Now I ask people to show me the thing working.

What actually drives it

The part that is not
about marketing at all

My kids are my world.

Being their dad is the best thing I do and the reason I build anything at all. Every late night on a site exists downstream of that, and if you ever have to choose between a deadline and your own kid, choose the kid. I will wait.

My faith is the frame.

I believe there is a purpose bigger than any business I will ever build, and that how you treat people on the way up is the actual scoreboard. It is why I will tell you not to buy something you do not need.

I paint, and I write poetry.

Abstracts and landscapes, mostly. It is where the color on this site comes from and the reason I refuse to build anything beige. A website that looks like every other website is a website nobody remembers.

Sales is the actual specialty.

You figured that out about four screens ago, probably. I am fine with that. Being sold to well is not an insult — it means someone bothered to understand what you needed before they opened their mouth.
