What an indie game developer can do for a business

Attention is the first sale. Play earns it.

A visitor who plays for three minutes remembers the name. A customer who walks through a 3D showroom understands the product without a brochure. A prospect who configures the thing on screen is halfway to buying it. Games and 3D aren't decoration when they're built for a job — they're the most memorable marketing a small business can own.

Branded games

Original, playable in a browser on any phone, with your product and message built into the mechanics — like the crossing game on this site, where the collectibles are the five things a business needs to be found.

3D showrooms and configurators

Walk-in stores, product tours, "build your own" tools. Customers see the thing from every angle, in your colors, before they call — and the page gets found, because it's built with search in mind.

VR-ready worlds

Everything I build runs on a screen and works with a headset. No app to install, no store to approve it. The World VR Mall is a live example with real storefronts.

How I build

Hand-coded, on the open web, built to be found.

I write the code myself — WebGL and canvas, no engine that locks you into a platform or a license. That keeps the experiences fast, light enough for a phone, and searchable: every 3D world I build ships with real pages, real text, structured data and the AI-search layer, so it's a marketing asset, not a toy that lives in an app store nobody visits.

Scope runs from a small branded game built into an existing site, through a 3D product showroom, up to a full VR world with storefronts, guides and tools. Everything is quoted per project after one conversation about what the experience has to do — remember, show, capture or teach.
