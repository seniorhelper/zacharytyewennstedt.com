        The visibility side of the job

        Five surfaces. One answer: you.

        Your buyer does not search anymore, they ask. Ranking on one surface out of five is not being found — it is being lucky.

        SURFACE 01

        Classic search

        The blue links. Still the largest single source of buying traffic, and still where trust gets built before anyone calls.

        SURFACE 02

        The map pack

        Three listings above everything else. For a local business this is the whole game, and it is won with profile work, reviews and proximity signals rather than blog posts.

        SURFACE 03

        AI Overviews

        Google answers the question before the links load. If you are not inside that answer, the click never happens at all.

        SURFACE 04

        Generative engines

        ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok. They name a handful of companies and move on. Being one of the handful is engineered, not accidental.

        SURFACE 05

        Agentic search

        An assistant shortlists vendors and acts for the buyer. Your site has to be readable by something that never once looks at your design.

        The questions I get asked

        A fractional CMO is a senior marketing leader you share instead of hire. I set the strategy, decide where the budget goes, build the systems that generate leads, train the people who answer the phone, and stay accountable for the number. The difference from an agency is ownership: an agency delivers a service, a CMO owns the outcome.

        An agency sells you a deliverable and reports on the deliverable. I am responsible for whether the phone rings. That means I will tell you to stop spending on something that is not working, even when that something is a line item you are paying me for. It also means I build in-house capability rather than making you dependent on me forever.

        It is quoted to the engagement: how many hours a month, how much of the build I am doing personally, and whether there is a team to manage. There is no published rate because a two-person shop and a twelve-state operation need completely different amounts of me. What I will say is that the whole point of fractional is that it costs a fraction of a full-time hire, and you can work out your own full-time number with the calculator on this page.

        Long enough to be useful. Ninety days is the minimum honest window to build systems and see whether they move, and most engagements run longer because compounding is the entire point. If you need a one-off project rather than ongoing leadership, say so and I will scope it that way instead.

        Usually the opposite. Most in-house marketers are perfectly capable and have simply never been given a strategy, a budget rationale or a senior person to check their thinking against. I would rather make your existing person better than make them redundant.

        No, and be very careful with anyone who does.
